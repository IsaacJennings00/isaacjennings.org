title: "The Perversion of Sisyphus"
date: 2026-10-11
---

Cold plunged this morning, followed by a coffee. Of course with 90 minutes between the two. Caffeine can't be taken any sooner after waking. The modafinil is swallowed and nicotine pouch parked. A podcast runs at low volume, Camus quoted without the surrounding argument. He tracks the next hour as deep work. The rock, as with every day, is the inbox, codebase, or the content calendar. The rock sits at the top of the hour, for five minutes or 15 minutes every hour. Then it rolls back down. Loving fate only happens when the metrics confirm it was worth the dose.

Camus ends [his essay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Myth_of_Sisyphus) by requiring us to imagine Sisyphus happy. The happiness is not the result of the climb. It is the conscious return down the mountain, the interval in which no meaning has been supplied and none can be extracted. The gods set the punishment; Sisyphus takes the rock as his own essence. The absurd is not solved. It is owned. Purpose is not discovered in a routine. It is decided, then used to fuel whatever project the person chooses to begin again.

[Grindmaxxing](https://www.reddit.com/r/Grindmaxxing/) takes the same figure and strips the interval. The descent becomes another block to be optimised. Stimulants and nootropics enter as technique: act first (dose, plunge, track), belief follows (I am the one who can sustain the myth). The “optimum” routine supplies the missing essence in advance. Existence no longer precedes it. The problem of the rock is treated as a solvable engineering task rather than the condition in which a person must still decide what the rock is for.

The myth functions here as founding story for continuous integration. It recruits the reader into participation—another dose, another protocol, another public display of the stack—then reshapes the participant into someone who experiences any unenhanced confrontation with the rock as failure. Scientism is the sacred ideology that makes the recruitment feel like clarity. Camus wrote in a moment without these tools. He did not need them. The perversion consists in using them to communicate a philosophy that rejected precisely this kind of external scaffolding.

What emerges is someone who can love the fate only after it has been measured and chemically supported. The tragedy remains—the rock still rolls—but the capacity to shape love and happiness inside it has been narrowed to what the protocol can register. Amor fati, if it is to be more than a caption, requires the refusal of new gods: the metric, the stack, the pre-written optimum. Without that refusal the person does not confront the absurd. He manages a simulation of it, and calls the management happiness.

One must imagine Sisyphus happy. The sentence does not survive the addition of the dose and the tracker. The rock is still there. The decision of what to do with the return down the mountain is still required. Everything else is a later costume for an older refusal to decide.
